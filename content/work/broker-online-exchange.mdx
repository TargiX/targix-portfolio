## What shipped

MyServiceCloud is the private platform behind **Broker Online Exchange**, used by energy brokers to manage customers, accounts, quoting, proposals, contracts, documents, and reporting. I led its frontend for close to five years: four at Broker Online Exchange and the final period after **AppDirect acquired the company**.

This was long-lived production ownership, not a greenfield handoff. The platform kept serving real business workflows while the team added product areas, modernized the stack, and moved the interface onto AppDirect's design language.

## My responsibility

I set frontend architecture and component patterns, reviewed code, mentored junior engineers, and decided how new product areas should be built on the client. I also worked across the API boundary with the PHP team and stayed responsible for whether the resulting flow was understandable, resilient, and shippable.

The recurring constraints were the useful kind:

- dense forms and tables with a large amount of interdependent state,
- multi-step flows where partial progress and validation had to remain legible,
- a mixed Vue and React frontend during modernization,
- and incremental releases on a platform the business could not pause.

## Modernizing the design system

After the acquisition, I led an incremental re-theme of MyServiceCloud onto **AppDirect's design language**. New areas used **React**, **Mantine**, and **Zustand** alongside the existing Vue application.

The important engineering decision was to make the migration additive and reviewable. Shared tokens and component patterns moved first; product areas followed in slices. That let the team improve consistency without freezing feature delivery or attempting a risky rewrite.

My role covered three connected layers:

- **System direction:** translating the new visual language into tokens, components, and repeatable interaction patterns.
- **Frontend architecture:** setting React structure and state patterns while maintaining stable boundaries with the Vue codebase.
- **Team adoption:** reviewing implementation, documenting decisions in code, and helping junior engineers ship within the system.

## Bill Scan to Quote

The most distinctive feature I led turned a scanned utility bill into a structured quote. A broker uploads a PDF or photo; **Gemini Vision** extracts line items and rate-plan data; the interface then makes that output reviewable and editable before it enters the quoting workflow.

The model call was only one part of the product. The frontend had to handle poor photos and multi-page bills, communicate extraction confidence, preserve the original source, and let a broker correct uncertain fields without starting over. The goal was not autonomous OCR. It was a faster human review loop that brokers could trust.

## Technical boundaries

- **Vue 3 + TypeScript** remained the production core.
- **React + Mantine + Zustand** powered newer product areas and the design-system migration.
- **Yii and Laravel** sat behind the API boundary during different phases of the platform.
- **Gemini Vision** provided structured extraction for Bill Scan to Quote.

## What this proves

Five years on one platform taught me how to keep a product coherent across changing requirements, multiple engineers, a design-system migration, and an acquisition. That is the work behind the title “Lead Frontend”: not only shipping a screen, but making the next screen easier for the team to ship well.
