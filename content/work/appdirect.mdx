## The role

For close to five years I led the frontend in AppDirect's Managed Services division. Not a single feature — the frontend. I set the direction for how we built UI, owned the architecture and component patterns, ran code review, and mentored the junior engineers on the team. When a new product area landed on the roadmap, I was the person who decided how it got built on the client and made sure it shipped.

The internal tooling itself (MyServiceCloud) isn't something I can put on a public page. But the platform that work powers — **Broker Online Exchange** — is public, and most of the UI in it is what my team and I shipped.

## Flagship: Bill Scan → Quote

The piece I'm proudest of is the flow that turns a **scanned telecom bill into a structured quote**. A broker uploads a PDF or photo of a customer's existing bill; the system reads it with **Gemini Vision** OCR, extracts the line items and rate plan, and turns that into a comparable quote inside the platform — instead of someone re-typing a bill by hand.

On the frontend that meant building an interface that could:

- handle messy real-world uploads (skewed scans, multi-page bills, bad photos) without falling over,
- show the OCR's confidence and let a human correct it before the numbers became a quote,
- and keep the whole review-and-correct loop fast enough that it actually saved brokers time.

It's the kind of feature where the AI is only half the work — the other half is the UI that makes the AI's output trustworthy and editable.

## Leading frontend, not just writing it

The part that doesn't show up in a screen recording: I was the senior frontend voice on the team for years.

- **Architecture & patterns.** Vue 3 + TypeScript on top of a Yii (PHP) backend. I owned how we structured components, state, and the API layer so the codebase stayed coherent as it grew across many product areas.
- **Code review.** I reviewed the frontend that shipped — the place where standards actually get enforced and where juniors learn the most.
- **Mentoring.** I brought junior engineers up: pairing, review feedback, and giving them ownership of real features with a safety net.
- **Design-engineering crossover.** With a UI/UX background, I could take a rough spec and make the interaction decisions myself instead of bouncing them back to design — which is how a lot of this shipped quickly.

## Stack

- **Vue 3 + TypeScript** — the whole frontend.
- **Yii (PHP)** — the backend the UI talked to.
- **Gemini Vision** — OCR for the bill-scanning flow.
- A dense component and pattern library built up over years to keep a large B2B app maintainable.

## What I take from it

Five years on one platform taught me the thing that's easy to skip on side projects: how to keep a real, growing product **coherent** — across many features, many engineers, and a long timeline — while still shipping. Leading the frontend there is why I'm comfortable being the senior frontend voice on a team, not just an individual contributor heads-down on a ticket.
